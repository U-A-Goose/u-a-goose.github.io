CMPUT 663: AI for Software Engineering

Term
Winter 2027
Instructor
Prof. Zhou Yang
Department

Course overview

This graduate course explores how AI is built and used for software engineering, with a focus on large language models of code and coding agents. It connects these systems to software engineering tasks and examines how to evaluate their usefulness, reliability, and limitations.

The course brings together software engineering and machine learning, including the security, privacy, and human factors that matter when AI is used in real software development.

Interested in research? For University of Alberta students interested in doing research with us, we strongly recommend taking this course.

Course timeline

15 teaching weeks · 2 classes per week · Reading Week excluded

  1. Week 01

    Introduction to Software Engineering

    This week introduces the goals, processes, tasks, and challenges of software engineering, covering requirements engineering, design, implementation, testing, deployment, and maintenance. Students will become familiar with software representations, formal specifications, testing, fuzzing, program analysis, agile development, and modern development tools, providing a foundation for understanding the engineering problems that AI can help address.

  2. Week 02

    Research Methods in Software Engineering

    This week introduces the research process in software engineering, including research questions and hypotheses, literature search and review, research design and evaluation, academic writing, and peer review. Students will learn to critically read existing work, assess research claims and supporting evidence, identify open problems, and develop an initial proposal for their group project.

  3. Week 03

    Mining Software Repositories and Software Analytics

    This week explores how source code, version histories, issue reports, code reviews, and execution logs can provide insights into software development and support task automation. Topics include data collection, feature and label design, classification, regression, clustering, and deep learning. Students will learn to construct research datasets and examine how data quality, sampling bias, and data leakage affect their findings.

  4. Week 04

    Code Representation Learning and Pretraining

    This week examines code representation learning and language model pretraining, covering tokenization, model architectures, training data, and pretraining objectives. Examples include code completion, masked language modeling, and fill-in-the-middle training. Students will explore how data and training objectives shape model capabilities, and learn to prepare training examples and conduct small-scale training experiments.

  5. Week 05

    Post-Training Code Models

    This week examines how pretrained code models are adapted to follow instructions, perform software engineering tasks, and learn from feedback. Topics include supervised fine-tuning and instruction tuning, preference optimization, knowledge distillation, and reinforcement learning with methods such as GRPO. Students will learn to design training examples and objectives, compare post-training methods, and investigate how learning signals affect model behavior and performance.

  6. Week 06

    Inference-Time Scaling

    This week explores how additional computation at inference time can improve performance on software engineering tasks. Topics include sampling multiple candidates, reasoning, search, iterative refinement, execution feedback, and verification. Students will learn to distinguish candidate generation from candidate selection, compare strategies under matched computational budgets, and analyze trade-offs among task success, latency, and computational cost.

  7. Week 07

    Coding Agents

    This week introduces agents that combine language models, tool use, and environmental feedback to perform software engineering tasks. Topics include task decomposition, repository exploration, planning, memory, and multi-agent collaboration. Through examples of fault localization, code modification, testing, and repair, students will learn to build a basic agent loop, analyze execution traces, and compare different workflows.

  8. Week 08

    Agent Harnesses

    This week examines agent harnesses, the software infrastructure that supports and controls agent execution. Topics include tool interfaces, execution environments, context management, state persistence, resource budgets, and failure handling and recovery. Students will learn how these choices affect the information, actions, and feedback available to an agent, and gain experience modifying harness components and evaluating their interaction with the model.

  9. Week 09

    AI for Software Engineering in Industry

    Industry practitioners will share their experience developing and deploying AI tools for software engineering. Discussions will examine how models and agents fit into development workflows, including code review and team collaboration, and how quality, cost, and operational constraints influence adoption. Students will use concrete cases to assess benefits and limitations, understand engineering bottlenecks, and identify research opportunities.

  10. Week 10

    Benchmarking and Evaluation

    This week examines how software engineering benchmarks are constructed and evaluated, including task selection, data splits, execution environments, and evaluation metrics. We will consider functional correctness, the runtime performance of generated software, and agent efficiency, alongside data leakage, test adequacy, variability, and the reliability of human and model-based evaluation. Students will learn to design reproducible comparisons and assess which conclusions the results support.

  11. Week 11

    Reliability, Security, and Privacy

    This week examines the reliability, security, and privacy of coding agents and generated software, as well as fairness requirements in relevant applications. Topics include secure code generation, prompt injection, unsafe tool use, access control, runtime verification, and human oversight. Students will learn to identify risks, evaluate mitigation strategies, and understand their guarantees, limitations, and effects on task completion.

  12. Week 12

    Applications in Hardware, Robotics, and Algorithm Optimization

    This week explores applications of code models and agents in hardware and integrated circuit design, robotics, and algorithm optimization. Through examples of domain adaptation, code generation, tool use, and verification, students will compare the domain knowledge, program representations, execution environments, and feedback required in each setting. They will examine where existing methods transfer and identify new engineering challenges.

  13. Week 13

    Research Project Presentations I

    The first set of groups will present their research projects and papers, covering the research questions, related work, methods, experimental results, and limitations. Through demonstrations, questions, and peer feedback, students will explain how the evidence supports their contributions, discuss reproducibility, and identify revisions needed to strengthen their work.

  14. Week 14

    Research Project Presentations II

    The remaining groups will present their research projects and papers, covering the research questions, related work, methods, experimental results, and limitations. Through demonstrations, questions, and peer feedback, students will explain how the evidence supports their contributions, discuss reproducibility, and identify revisions needed to strengthen their work.

  15. Week 15

    Project Revisions and Research Artifacts

    Groups will present their revisions and respond to feedback from earlier sessions. Discussions will focus on additional evidence, changes to methods or conclusions, remaining limitations, and reproducibility. Students will refine their papers and research artifacts, justify their final claims, and identify the work needed before submission to a research venue.
